The Gospel

Gospel means good news. Not good advice, not a better plan for your life — news. Something happened, outside of you, before you had any say in it, and everything turns on whether it is true.

God Is Holy, and He Made You

He is not a force or a feeling. He is a Person, and He made you on purpose. Everything you have — breath, mind, the ability to read this — is His. That means He has a claim on you, and He is not indifferent to what you do with what He gave.

He is also holy. He does not overlook evil, and a judge who shrugged at it would not be good.

We Are Not

“For all did sin, and are come short of the glory of God” (Romans 3:23). Not most. All. This is not about being worse than your neighbor; it is about the standard being God Himself.

And sin is not a small debt. “For the wages of the sin is death, and the gift of God is life age-during in Christ Jesus our Lord” (Romans 6:23). Death is what sin earns. It is wages — something owed.

That is the problem no amount of self-improvement touches. You cannot pay off a debt with money you already owe.

What God Did About It

He did not wait for us to clean ourselves up. “And God doth commend His own love to us, that, in our being still sinners, Christ did die for us” (Romans 5:8). While we were still in it. Not after we improved.

And the exchange at the center of it: “for him who did not know sin, in our behalf He did make sin, that we may become the righteousness of God in him” (2 Corinthians 5:21). Christ took what was ours. We receive what was His. He got the debt; we got the standing.

Then He did not stay dead. “Christ died for our sins, according to the Writings, and that he was buried, and that he hath risen on the third day, according to the Writings” (1 Corinthians 15:3–4). The resurrection is the receipt. It is God saying the payment cleared.

You Cannot Earn This

“For by grace ye are having been saved, through faith, and this not of you — of God the gift, not of works, that no one may boast” (Ephesians 2:8–9).

A gift you worked for is a wage. The moment you add your résumé to it, it stops being grace. Nobody gets to walk in with a list of what they did.

What He Asks

Two things, and they are one motion. “Reform ye, and believe in the good news” (Mark 1:15). Turn, and trust.

Repentance is not feeling bad. It is changing your mind about who is in charge and turning around. Faith is not optimism. It is resting the whole weight of your standing before God on what Christ did instead of on what you did.

“That if thou mayest confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and mayest believe in thy heart that God did raise him out of the dead, thou shalt be saved” (Romans 10:9).

What Happens Then

You are not put on probation. You are made new and taken in.

“So that if any one is in Christ — he is a new creature; the old things did pass away, lo, become new have the all things” (2 Corinthians 5:17).

“But as many as did receive him to them he gave authority to become sons of God — to those believing in his name” (John 1:12). Not a servant kept at arm's length. A son, with a Father.

And the verdict is already in: “There is, then, now no condemnation to those in Christ Jesus” (Romans 8:1). Now. Not pending.

It is not a grip you have to maintain. “And life age-during I give to them, and they shall not perish — to the age, and no one shall pluck them out of my hand” (John 10:28). He is holding you.

If You Want In

There is no form to fill out and no ritual to perform. Tell Him. Admit what you are, ask Him for what you cannot earn, and put your trust in Christ rather than in yourself. Then find people who believe this and open the Book with them.

I did not find my way in. I was placed in it, on purpose, before there was a world to place me in. If that is true of me, it can be true of you.
